## SIMPLE & COMPOUND INTEREST

### 1. Difference between SI and CI

**Question:** Find the difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹4000 in 3 years at 20% per annum.

**Ans:-**

- Simple-interest percentage for 3 years: **SI% = 20 x 3 = 60%**
- Compound-interest percentage for 3 years: **CI% = 72.8%**
- Difference in percentage: **72.8 - 60 = 12.8%**

$$
\text{Difference} = 4000 \times \frac{12.8}{100} = \text{₹}512
$$

**Final Answer: ₹512**

### 2. Finding the Principal from the Difference between CI and SI

**Question:** The difference between CI and SI on a certain sum in 3 years at 10% per annum is ₹155. Find the sum.

**Ans:-**

> **Long Method:**
>
> Let the principal be $P$.
>
> $$
> P\left(1+\frac{10}{100}\right)^3 - P - \frac{P \times 10 \times 3}{100} = 155
> $$
>
> $$
> P\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^3 - P - \frac{3P}{10} = 155
> $$
>
> $$
> P\left(\frac{1331}{1000}\right) - P - \frac{3P}{10} = 155
> $$
>
> $$
> P\left(\frac{1331 - 1000 - 300}{1000}\right) = 155
> $$
>
> $$
> P\left(\frac{31}{1000}\right) = 155
> $$
>
> $$
> P = \frac{155 \times 1000}{31} = 5000
> $$

**Therefore, the principal is ₹5000.**

  <img src="https://github.com/PSCodersHub/PLACEMENT/raw/main/Assets/100.jpeg" alt="Long method for finding the principal when the CI and SI difference is ₹155" width="500">
</div>

> **Shortcut Method (Percentage Concept):**
>
> - SI percentage for 3 years: **10 x 3 = 30%**
> - CI percentage for 3 years: **33.1%**
> - Difference: **33.1 - 30 = 3.1%**
>
> Therefore:
>
> 3.1% = $\text{₹}155$
>
> 100% = $\frac{155 \times 100}{3.1} = \text{₹}5000$

**Final Answer: ₹5000**

### 3. Finding CI when SI is Given

**Question:** The simple interest on a certain sum in 3 years at 10% per annum is ₹1500. What is the compound interest on the same sum for the same number of years at the same rate?

(a) ₹1665 &nbsp; (b) ₹1650 &nbsp; (c) ₹1655 &nbsp; (d) ₹1555

**Ans:-**

- SI percentage: **10 x 3 = 30%**
- CI percentage: **33.1%**

If 30% of the principal is ₹1500:

100% = $\frac{1500 \times 100}{30} = \text{₹}5000$

Now calculate 33.1% of ₹5000:

$CI = 5000 \times \frac{33.1}{100} = \text{₹}1655$

**Correct Option: (c) ₹1655**

## RATIO, PROPORTION & VARIATION

### Fundamentals of Ratio

A ratio compares quantities of the same kind. If the ratio of two numbers is $a:b$, their actual values can be represented as $ax$ and $bx$, where $x$ is the common multiplying factor.

To solve a ratio question, use the information given to find $x$, then calculate the required quantity.

#### Fundamental Property of Ratios

Multiplying or dividing every term of a ratio by the same non-zero number does not change the ratio.

For example:

$$
\frac{1}{2}:\frac{1}{3} = 3:2
$$

Multiplying both terms by 6 removes the denominators. Similarly:

$$
\frac{1}{2}:\frac{1}{3}:\frac{1}{5} = 15:10:6
$$

Here, every term is multiplied by 30, the LCM of 2, 3 and 5.

### 4. Division of an Amount in a Given Ratio

**Question:** ₹300 is divided among A, B and C in the ratio $2:3:5$. What is the share of B?

(a) ₹70 &nbsp; (b) ₹80 &nbsp; (c) ₹90 &nbsp; (d) ₹100

**Ans:-**

Actual shares of A, B and C are $2x$, $3x$ and $5x$.

$$
2x + 3x + 5x = 300
$$

$$
10x = 300 \Rightarrow x = 30
$$

B's share:

$$
3x = 3 \times 30 = \text{₹}90
$$

**Correct Option: (c) ₹90**

### 5. Finding the Total Amount from the Difference between Shares

**Question:** A certain amount is divided among A, B and C in the ratio $2:3:5$. B's share is ₹60 less than C's share. Find the total amount.

(a) ₹250 &nbsp; (b) ₹300 &nbsp; (c) ₹900 &nbsp; (d) ₹600

**Ans:-**

Let the shares be $2x$, $3x$ and $5x$.

The difference between C's and B's shares is ₹60:

$$
5x - 3x = 60
$$

$$
2x = 60 \Rightarrow x = 30
$$

Total amount:

$$
2x + 3x + 5x = 10x = 10 \times 30 = \text{₹}300
$$

**Correct Option: (b) ₹300**

### 6. Ratio after Successive Percentage Changes

**Question:** The ratio of boys to girls in a class was $5:3$ last week. This week, the number of boys increases by 10% and the number of girls decreases by 10%. What is the new ratio of boys to girls?

**Ans:-**

The numbers of boys and girls last week can be represented as $5x$ and $3x$.

New number of boys:

$$
5x \times \frac{110}{100}
$$

New number of girls:

$$
3x \times \frac{90}{100}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{New ratio} = \frac{5x \times 110}{3x \times 90} = \frac{550}{270} = \boxed{55:27}
$$

**Answer: $55:27$**

> **Shortcut Method:**
>
> An increase of 10% means multiplying by 1.1, while a decrease of 10% means multiplying by 0.9.
>
> $$
> \text{New ratio} = \frac{5 \times 1.1}{3 \times 0.9} = \frac{5 \times 110}{3 \times 90} = \boxed{55:27}
> $$

**Question:** The ratio of boys to girls in a class was $5:3$. The number of boys increases by 20% and the number of girls decreases by 30%. Find the new ratio.

## 8. *Different Types of RATIO*:

### i. Duplicate Ratio

The **duplicate ratio** of $a:b$ is the ratio of the squares of its terms.

$$
\text{Duplicate ratio of } a:b = a^2:b^2
$$

**Example:** Find the duplicate ratio of $2:3$.

$$
2^2:3^2 = 4:9
$$

**Answer: $4:9$**

### ii. Triplicate Ratio

The **triplicate ratio** of $a:b$ is the ratio of the cubes of its terms.

$$
\text{Triplicate ratio of } a:b = a^3:b^3
$$

**Example:** Find the triplicate ratio of $2:3$.

$$
2^3:3^3 = 8:27
$$

**Answer: $8:27$**

### iii. Sub-Duplicate Ratio

The **sub-duplicate ratio** is obtained by taking the square root of each term of the given ratio.

$$
\text{Sub-duplicate ratio of } a:b = \sqrt{a}:\sqrt{b}
$$

**Example:** Find the sub-duplicate ratio of $4:9$.

$$
\sqrt{4}:\sqrt{9} = 2:3
$$

**Answer: $2:3$**

### iv. Sub-Triplicate Ratio

The **sub-triplicate ratio** is obtained by taking the cube root of each term of the given ratio.

$$
\text{Sub-triplicate ratio of } a:b = \sqrt[3]{a}:\sqrt[3]{b}
$$

**Example:** Find the sub-triplicate ratio of $8:27$.

$$
\sqrt[3]{8}:\sqrt[3]{27} = 2:3
$$

**Answer: $2:3$**

### v. Compound Ratio

The **compound ratio** is obtained by multiplying the antecedents together and the consequents together.

For the ratios $a:b$, $c:d$ and $e:f$:

$$
\text{Compound ratio} = ace:bdf
$$

**Question:** Find the compound ratio of $2:3$, $3:4$ and $5:6$.

$$
(2 \times 3 \times 5):(3 \times 4 \times 6)
$$

$$
= 30:72 = \boxed{5:12}
$$

**Answer: $5:12$**

### vi. Inverse / Reciprocal Ratio

The **inverse or reciprocal ratio** is obtained by taking the reciprocals of the terms of the given ratio.

For non-zero terms:

$$
\text{Inverse ratio of } a:b = \frac{1}{a}:\frac{1}{b} = b:a
$$

**Example:** Find the inverse ratio of $2:3$.

$$
\frac{1}{2}:\frac{1}{3} = 3:2
$$

**Answer: $3:2$**

### vi-a. Inverse Ratio of Three Terms

**Question:** Find the inverse ratio of $2:3:4$.

**Method 1 — Take the reciprocals:**

$$
\frac{1}{2}:\frac{1}{3}:\frac{1}{4}
$$

The LCM of 2, 3 and 4 is 12. Multiplying every term by 12 gives:

$$
\frac{1}{2}:\frac{1}{3}:\frac{1}{4} = 6:4:3
$$

**Answer: $6:4:3$**

> **Shortcut Method:**
>
> Find the LCM of the terms and divide it by each term.
>
> $$
> LCM(2,3,4) = 12
> $$
>
> $$
> 12 \div 2:12 \div 3:12 \div 4 = \boxed{6:4:3}
> $$

### vi-c. When the New Ratio Cannot Be Determined

**Question:** The ratio of boys to girls in a class was $5:3$ last year. This year, the number of boys increases by 20 and the number of girls increases by 30. What is the ratio of boys to girls this year?

**Ans:-**

Let the numbers last year be $5x$ and $3x$.

The numbers this year become:

$$
\text{Boys} = 5x + 20
$$

$$
\text{Girls} = 3x + 30
$$

Therefore, the new ratio is:

$$
(5x + 20):(3x + 30)
$$

Since the value of $x$ is not given, the new ratio cannot be uniquely determined.

**Answer: Cannot be determined.**

### vii. Final Ratio

### vii-a. Final Ratio of Three Quantities — Method 1

**Question:** If $a:b = 6:7$ and $b:c = 8:9$, find $a:b:c$.

**Ans:-**

The common term $b$ has values 7 and 8. Make these values equal using their LCM:

$$
LCM(7,8) = 56
$$

Multiply the first ratio by 8:

$$
a:b = 6:7 = 48:56
$$

Multiply the second ratio by 7:

$$
b:c = 8:9 = 56:63
$$

Combining the terms:

$$
\boxed{a:b:c = 48:56:63}
$$

### vii-b. Final Ratio of Three Quantities — Method 2

**Question:** If $a:b = 2:3$ and $b:c = 4:5$, find $a:b:c$.

### vii-c. Final Ratio of Four Quantities

**Question:** If $a:b = 1:3$, $b:c = 2:5$ and $c:d = 1:4$, find $a:b:c:d$.

**Ans:-**

First, make the common term $b$ equal in the first two ratios. The LCM of 3 and 2 is 6:

$$
a:b = 1:3 = 2:6
$$

$$
b:c = 2:5 = 6:15
$$

Now make $c = 15$ in the third ratio:

$$
c:d = 1:4 = 15:60
$$

Combining all four terms:

$$
\boxed{a:b:c:d = 2:6:15:60}
$$

### 9. Proportion — Fundamental Concept

When two ratios are equal, they form a **proportion**.

$$
\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}
$$

It can also be written as:

$$
a:b::c:d
$$

In the proportion $a:b::c:d$:

- $a$ and $d$ are called the **extremes**.
- $b$ and $c$ are called the **means**.
- $a$ is the first proportional.
- $b$ is the second proportional.
- $c$ is the third proportional.
- $d$ is the fourth proportional.

**Question:** Find the third proportional between 2, 3 and 15.

The slide uses the following equation:

$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{x}{15}
$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$
3x = 2 \times 15 = 30
$$

$$
x = \frac{30}{3} = 10
$$

**Answer using the equation shown: $x = 10$**

> **Terminology note:** The slide's wording does not match the usual terminology for proportional terms. The displayed equation $2:3 = x:15$ gives $x = 10$, and that equation and answer are retained here as shown. In standard terminology:
>
> - The third proportional to 2 and 3 satisfies $2:3 = 3:x$, giving $x = 4.5$.
> - The fourth proportional to 2, 3 and 15 satisfies $2:3 = 15:x$, giving $x = 22.5$.

**Question:** Find the fourth proportional to 4, 5 and 12.

$$
\frac{4}{5} = \frac{12}{x}

Cross-multiplying:

$$

### 9-c. Continued Proportion — Mean Proportional

In a continued proportion, three terms satisfy:

$$
\frac{a}{b} = \frac{b}{c}
$$

The middle term is the mean proportional. The means remain the same in the two ratios.

**Question:** Find the mean proportional between 9 and 25.

**Ans:-**

Let the mean proportional be $x$.

$$
\frac{9}{x} = \frac{x}{25}
$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$
x^2 = 9 \times 25 = 225
$$

$$
x = \sqrt{225} = 15
$$

**Answer: 15**

### 9-d. Third Proportional

**Question:** Find the third proportional to 8 and 10.

**Ans:-**

Let the third proportional be $x$. By definition:

$$
8:10 = 10:x
$$

$$
\frac{8}{10} = \frac{10}{x}
$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$
8x = 10 \times 10 = 100
$$

$$
x = \frac{100}{8} = 12.5
$$

**Answer: 12.5**

### 10. Age Problems

**Question:** The ratio between the present ages of Ram and Shyam is $3:5$. After 5 years, the ratio will become $2:3$. Find their present ages.

**Ans:-**

Let the present ages of Ram and Shyam be $3x$ and $5x$, respectively.

After 5 years, their ages will be $3x + 5$ and $5x + 5$.

According to the question:

$$
\frac{3x + 5}{5x + 5} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

Cross-multiplying:

$$
3(3x + 5) = 2(5x + 5)
$$

$$
9x + 15 = 10x + 10
$$

$$
x = 5
$$

Present age of Ram:

$$
3x = 3 \times 5 = 15 \text{ years}
$$

Present age of Shyam:

$$
5x = 5 \times 5 = 25 \text{ years}
$$

**Final Answer: Ram is 15 years old and Shyam is 25 years old.**
