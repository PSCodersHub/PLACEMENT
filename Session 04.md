### Concept - 2: Base Given, Result No Given, What % is the result of the base required?

#### Percentage Calculation: Fraction Method

**Example 1:**

* $11.11$% of $81 = 9$
* What % is $9$ of $81$? $\longrightarrow \frac{9}{81} \times 100 = 11.11$%
* Using fraction: $\frac{1}{9} \times 100 = 11.11$%

**Example 2:**

* $66.66$% of $240 = 160$
* What % of $240$ is $160$? $\longrightarrow \frac{160}{240} \times 100 = 66.66$%
* Using fraction: $\frac{2}{3} \times 100 = 66.66$%

### Simple Interest Questions

**3. At what rate % will the simple interest on Rs. 6000 in 4 years will be Rs 1200.**  
(a) 5% &nbsp; &nbsp; (b) 4% &nbsp; &nbsp; (c) 6% &nbsp; &nbsp; (d) 8%

**Ans:-**

> **Decoding the Question: What is it actually asking?**  
> Imagine you deposit **₹6000** (the Principal/Base) into a bank. After leaving it there for **4 years**, the bank rewards you with **₹1200** in extra money (the Simple Interest).
> The question is asking: *"What percentage of your original ₹6000 is the bank giving you every single year?"*

> **Concept Note: Why calculate the 1-year value?**  
> The interest rate (R%) is always calculated "per annum" (yearly). In Simple Interest, the amount of interest earned remains exactly the same every year. By breaking the total interest down to a single year, we can directly find what percentage of the Principal is being added annually.

First, find the simple interest for 1 year:

* Simple Interest for 4 years = 1200
* Simple Interest for 1 year = $\frac{1200}{4} = 300$

Now, calculate what percentage this 1-year interest is of the principal (Base):
Rate % = $\frac{300}{6000} \times 100 = 5$%

**Correct Option: (a)**

**5. A batsman scored 120 runs out of which there are 5 boundaries and 5 sixes. What percentage runs he scored by running between the wickets?**  
(a) 50% &nbsp; &nbsp; (b) 55% &nbsp; &nbsp; (c) 52% &nbsp; &nbsp; (d) 58.33%

**Ans:-**

* Runs from boundaries (4s): $5 \times 4 = 20$
* Runs from sixes (6s): $5 \times 6 = 30$
* Total runs from boundaries and sixes = $20 + 30 = 50$

Runs scored by running between the wickets = $120 - 50 = 70$ runs.

Percentage = $\frac{70}{120} \times 100 = 58.33$%

Alternatively, using fractions: $\frac{7}{12} = \frac{6}{12} + \frac{1}{12}$ = 50% + 8.33% = 58.33%

**Correct Option: (d)**

### Usage of words like OF, More/less than, increased / decreased by...

Understanding the phrasing is crucial for percentage calculation:

* **What % is 15 of 20?**  
    $\frac{15}{20} \times 100 = 75$%  (Fraction: $\frac{3}{4} = 75$%)
* **What % is 20 of 15?**  
    $\frac{20}{15} \times 100 = 133.33$% (Fraction: $\frac{4}{3} = 1\frac{1}{3} = 133.33$%)
* **What % is 20 more than 15?**  
    Difference = 5. Base = 15.  
    $\frac{5}{15} \times 100 = 33.33$% (Fraction: $\frac{1}{3} = 33.33$%)
* **What % is 15 less than 20?**  
    Difference = 5. Base = 20.  
    $\frac{5}{20} \times 100 = 25$% (Fraction: $\frac{1}{4} = 25$%)

  <img src="https://github.com/PSCodersHub/PLACEMENT/raw/main/Assets/22.jpg" alt="Usage of Words 1" width="500">
</div>

**What percent is 30 of 40?**  
Base = 40 (the number after "of"), Value = 30  
$\frac{30}{40} \times 100 = 75$%

**What percent is 40 of 30?**  
Base = 30 (the number after "of"), Value = 40 — base and value are swapped from the previous question, which is why the answer isn't just the reciprocal-looking 75%  
$\frac{40}{30} \times 100 = 133.33$%

**What percent is 40 more than 25?**  
"More than" → Base = 25 (the number being compared *to*), Difference = $40 - 25 = 15$  
$\frac{15}{25} \times 100 = 60$%

**What percent is 40 less than 48?**  
"Less than" → Base = 48 (the number being compared *to*), Difference = $48 - 40 = 8$  
$\frac{8}{48} \times 100 = 16.66$%

> **Why the base flips:**  
> For "X of Y," the base is always Y (whatever comes right after "of"). For "X more/less than Y," the base is always Y (the number being compared against), and the numerator is just the plain difference between the two numbers — never divided by X.

### Comparison of 2 numbers: Percentage increase / decrease

**Formulas:**

* **% INCREASE =** $\frac{\text{Increase value}}{\text{Original / previous value}} \times 100$
* **% DECREASE =** $\frac{\text{Decrease value}}{\text{Original / previous value}} \times 100$
* **% CHANGE =** $\frac{\text{Change (Increase or Decrease) value}}{\text{Original / previous value}} \times 100$

**6. Sale of an item was 200 units last month. This month it becomes 250. Find the percentage increase.**

**Ans:-**

* Original/Previous Value = 200
* New Value = 250
* Increase Value = $250 - 200 = 50$

% Increase = $\frac{50}{200} \times 100 = 25$%

### Finding rate % at Compound Interest

**11. At what rate of compound interest per annum will a sum of Rs.3000 become Rs. 3993 in 3 yr?**  
(a) 8% &nbsp; &nbsp; (b) 12% &nbsp; &nbsp; (c) 10% &nbsp; &nbsp; (d) 6%

**Ans:-**

**Method 1: Formula (Time-Consuming - Avoid)**  
$AMOUNT = P(1 + \frac{R}{100})^N$  
$3000(1 + \frac{r}{100})^3 = 3993$  
$(1 + \frac{r}{100})^3 = \frac{3993}{3000} = \frac{1331}{1000}$  
$(1 + \frac{r}{100})^3 = (\frac{11}{10})^3$  
$(1 + \frac{r}{100}) = \frac{11}{10} \implies \frac{r}{100} = \frac{1}{10} \implies r = 10$%

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://github.com/PSCodersHub/PLACEMENT/raw/main/Assets/30.jpg" alt="Compound Interest Formula" width="500">
</div>

**Method 2: Hit & Trial Method (Recommended)**  
Total Interest Earned = $3993 - 3000 = Rs. 993$

Let's check the options for Simple Interest approximation:

* **Option a (8%):** 8% of 3000 = 240. In 3 years SI = $240 \times 3 = 720$. (Too low compared to 993).
* **Option b (12%):** 12% of 3000 = 360. In 3 years SI = $360 \times 3 = 1080$. (Exceeds 993, so CI will be even higher).
* **Option c (10%):** 10% of 3000 = 300. In 3 years SI = $300 \times 3 = 900$. (Close to 993, the extra 93 accounts for compounding).

Hence, Rate = **10%**

**Correct Option: (c)**
